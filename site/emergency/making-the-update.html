Making the HPAI update

Emergency Response dashboard · for staff who make the monthly HPAI update · also as MAKING_THE_HPAI_UPDATE.txt beside this page

The dashboard makes the monthly HPAI update for you. You load the HPAI table, and the dashboard counts Washington's figures and draws the pages. You type only what comes from other places (USDA's and WADDL's numbers) and the words that change from month to month. Then you save it as a PDF.

It takes about 20 minutes once you have last month's file. Nothing you load or type leaves your computer.

1. Before you start

Have these ready:

  • The HPAI table, saved as a .csv file (in Excel: File, Save As, CSV).
  • Last month's report text, the small file you saved last month, named like HPAI update 2026-09-01 report text.json. The first time, you start without one.
  • USDA's and WADDL's pages, open in other browser tabs, for the national, wild bird and testing numbers. USDA's addresses are in the National (USDA) fold's address boxes; WADDL's are in the Wild birds and testing (WADDL) fold's link lists.

2. Open the Report view

  1. Open the Emergency Response dashboard and load the HPAI table under Load your data.
  2. Above the map, press Report (beside Map and Overview).
  3. At the top, Fill in is pressed: the form is on the left and the update's pages are on the right. The pages change as you type.
The pages always count the whole table, whatever the map's "Animals" ticks or dates are set to.

3. Each month, step by step

The form is in folds. Click a fold's heading to open or close it. Work from the top down.

1Load last month's report text

Press Load last month's at the bottom of the form and pick last month's report text. It fills in the words, the key messages, the boxes and the links. USDA's and WADDL's numbers and pictures stay empty for the new month, but last month's numbers are kept to compare with.

To go on with a draft you saved this month, set the Report date to the draft's date first, then load it: its numbers then come back too.

2Report date

Set the date the update is for. Every figure is counted as on that date; a row detected after it is left out.

3Washington: the status

Read the line "From the data on ..." at the top of the fold. It says how many active positives and zones there are. Then pick the status sentence that fits it. If your pick doesn't fit the data, the fold says Check and explains why. Pick Write my own only when no approved wording fits.

Also pick the milk testing stage, and tick Suspected livestock cases are being tested only when that's true.

4Washington: from the data

Nothing to type: Washington's numbers fill themselves in. Choose the map's look: Soft dots (no exact place) or Counties shaded by cases. Neither shows a real farm's place. If you know of a gap (for example, cases not yet entered in the table), write it in the optional note. You can't type over the numbers; the note is the way to explain one.

5Key messages

Each message has a headline, an icon, its words and an optional link. Tick Highlight to shade a card. Use the arrows to move a message, Set aside to keep it for another month without printing it, and + Add a message for a new one. Icon style (Filled or Outline) changes every card at once. The livestock card comes after your messages; it is made from the data.

6National (USDA)

Copy each number from USDA's page into its box, with USDA's As of date. Don't type the flocks in all; the form adds them up.

For USDA's maps, last 30 days, add a picture of each of USDA's three maps: press Add USDA's picture and pick a saved picture, or take a snip of the map (on Windows, Windows key + Shift + S), click the box and press Ctrl+V.

7Wild birds and testing (WADDL)

Type Washington's wild bird and wild mammal numbers. If there were none, tick "No wild bird detections reported in the last 30 days" (or the mammal one) instead. Under Testing (WADDL), type the PCR tests with WADDL's As of date and the number for each category.

8Boxes and resources

The line under the title, the hotline, the "Report online at" address, the alternate-format line and the link lists. They stay the same from month to month; change them only when they change.

9Save your work

  • ↓ Save report text saves everything you typed in one small file (nothing from the table). Keep it on the R drive; next month you load it in step 1.
  • ↓ Save for ArcGIS saves page 2's numbers as hpai-monthly-figures.csv with the Washington sentences, for the ArcGIS dashboard's monthly table, so you type them only once.
The pictures are not kept in the report text. Each month starts without them.

4. What the fold headings tell you

Each fold's heading says how it stands, so you can see what's left without opening every fold:

The heading saysWhat it means
Matches the data Automatic All filledNothing to do.
Kept month to month 3 messagesFine as it is; look it over.
Check 2 to checkOpen the fold: a box with an orange edge says what looks wrong under it.
15 not filled · 3 picturesBoxes still empty and pictures still to add. An empty box prints "not filled", never last month's number.

The form checks the numbers you type. It says so when:

  • an "As of" date is missing, more than a month old, or after the report's date;
  • a word is typed where a number goes;
  • a last-30-days number is larger than its total;
  • a total is lower than last month's, or the same as in the last two reports;
  • an address would not open.

A check is a reminder, not a stop: if the number really is right, leave it.

5. Print or save the PDF

  1. Every page says DRAFT until you tick Ready to send (beside the print button). If something is still orange, the line beside it names the folds.
  2. Press Print / Save as PDF.
  3. In the print window, choose Save as PDF as the printer (Destination) and save. The file name is already "HPAI update" and the date.

Only the update's pages print, at letter size. If the colors are missing, tick "Background graphics" under More settings.

6. Figures: the update's figures on screen

Press Figures at the top of the Report view to read page 1's figures on screen. It is the fallback if the ArcGIS dashboard is down.

  • Rest the pointer on any number, bar or month to see it and how it is counted.
  • Click a county or a month to list only its cases in the table. Click it again (or the × beside its name) to see this year's cases again.
  • The map's Places | Counties: Places shows each case at its real place, with the active ones' 3 and 10 km zones. Figures is for staff only; it never prints. Printing from Figures prints the update's pages.

The Overview's link The update's figures: Report opens Figures too.

7. How the update counts

The update counts a little differently from the map and the Overview, so it matches the update as it has always been published:

  • Every row is a case. A premises detected twice counts twice; the update says how many were detected more than once.
  • A row without a map point still counts, in its county. It is left off only the map.
  • A row detected after the report's date is left out, so an older update can be made again as it read then. A row without a readable date counts in the totals but in no month.
  • Flock size: Small under 100 birds, Medium 100 to 999, Large 1,000 or more.
  • Active surveillance areas are the zones around the cases active on the report's date.

8. When something looks wrong

What you seeWhat to do
No Report buttonLoad the HPAI table first. The button shows only for an emergency that has an update (today, HPAI).
A Washington number looks wrongCheck the report date first. Then use Figures: rest the pointer on the number to see how it is counted, and click its county or month to list the cases behind it. A wrong number comes from the table, so fix the table and load it again.
"Load last month's" says a part was not keptThe file was saved by an older version or changed by hand. The form keeps what it could read and names the rest; fill those in again.
A picture won't go inClick the picture's box first, then press Ctrl+V. A picture over 10 MB, or a file that isn't a picture, is refused and the box says so.
